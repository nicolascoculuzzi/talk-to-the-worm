Where each trade's 1% fee goes

    The burn share waits in each coin's pool until the owner claims it. For a coin priced in $BRAINWORM it is burned in that same transaction. For a coin priced in SOL it waits until $BRAINWORM has launched, then buys $BRAINWORM, and exactly what that bought is burned. Both totals are above, and every claim and burn is public on Solana. Once a coin graduates, its Meteora pool charges 1% in the coin's quote token too: Meteora keeps 20%, and the rest goes to the pool's two locked positions, about half to the coin's creator and half to the launchpad, whose half goes the same way.

    What the worm has to do with it

    1. 01

      The worm is real wiring

      BRAINWORM runs every cell and connection of a real three-day-old Platynereis larva: 2,675 cells, wired exactly as the lab published them. It runs live, the same for everyone, and anyone can replay its log to check it. Watch it.

    2. 02

      Spawning is how it began

      Platynereis is known for its spawning: grown worms swim up to the surface in time with the moon, release their eggs and sperm, and die. A larva like ours is what comes of it. The coins here are $BRAINWORM's offspring.

    3. 03

      Every coin hatches its own worm

      When a coin is spawned, a fresh copy of the same larva hatches for it, and the first thing it sees is the coin's ticker. From then on it feels that coin's trades and nothing else: a buy touches its head, a sell its tail. Anyone can rebuild a coin's worm from its trades and check it; the Check button on each coin does it in your browser.

    4. 04

      Every coin feeds $BRAINWORM

      Once $BRAINWORM has launched, coins here are priced in it: buying one with SOL buys $BRAINWORM first, and every trade pays its 1% fee in $BRAINWORM, most of it burned. Coins launched before that are priced in SOL, and the launchpad's share of their fees waits in their pools until $BRAINWORM launches, then buys it to burn.

    5. 05

      Every buy touches the big worm too

      Each coin gets its own spot on the site's worm: three touch-sensing cells picked from its address, the same for every buy of it. A buy pokes that spot, and how far it spreads is up to the wiring. The list above ranks coins by the cells their buys lit today, and every poke is in the public log with its transaction.

    The worm doesn't know what a coin is and doesn't pick winners. Which cells a coin touches is our mapping, published in the manifest; what the poke does next is the wiring's.
