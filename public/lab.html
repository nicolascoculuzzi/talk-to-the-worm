The whole larva

Tests written down before they ran

One live simulation of a real Platynereis larva's published wiring: 2,675 cells, 26,881 synapses. Each test's rule was written down before its first run. It is never edited: a change is a new version. Each test runs on the real wiring and on 50 randomly rewired copies, where every cell keeps its number of connections but who connects to whom is scrambled. A test passes only if the real wiring beats 95% of them. A failed test stays here, marked failed.

Limits

What the model is not

  • A simple model runs it. Firing-rate cells. Each connection's strength is its synapse count, scaled so the wiring can't amplify itself. Known transmitters decide when the cilia stop. Nothing trained. Simplified
  • The model is simpler than the animal. The transmitter is known for 79 of 2,675 cells, so almost every synapse is excitatory. Real neurons spike, inhibit and modulate.
  • It can't read. Your message reaches it only as light. It understands nothing.
  • It doesn't choose. A tug shows which way its body turned, not a preference. Nothing here predicts prices.

Stated plainly

The parts that aren't the worm

  • Your message becomes light. Drawn in a pixel font, one column per step. Each eye cell gets its strip's brightness; which strip is our choice. Chosen
  • Some choices are ours. The eye mapping, the light sensors' threshold, the poke cells, the swimming physics and the drawing.
  • Positions are partly placed. 1,199 cells at traced positions, 1,009 placed by segment and side, 467 fragments not drawn.
  • The body is ours. The glass outline, the swimming physics and the lamp are ours. Only the lamp feeds the body back into the brain.
  • Coins aren't the worm's. A SPAWN coin's buys poke it at a spot we pick from the coin's address, and each coin's own worm is a copy fed only that coin's trades. The worm knows nothing about coins or prices, and it doesn't pick winners.
