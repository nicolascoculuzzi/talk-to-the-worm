      How it works · in 30 seconds

      A real worm's brain, a copy for every coin

      1. 01

        The worm is real

        BRAINWORM runs a simulation of a real sea worm larva's whole nervous system: all 2,675 cells and 26,881 synapses, exactly as scientists mapped them (eLife, 2025). Nothing invented, nothing trained. One copy runs live, and everyone watching sees the same worm.

        How the digital worm was made →

      2. 02

        Every coin hatches its own

        Launch a coin and a fresh copy of that worm hatches for it. The first thing it sees is your ticker. After that it feels every trade on your coin's bonding curve, and nothing else: a buy touches its head, a sell its tail.

      3. 03

        Every trade feeds $BRAINWORM

        Coins trade on a Meteora bonding curve. Each trade pays 1%: a fifth to Meteora, a fifth of the rest to the coin's creator, and the rest goes to burning $BRAINWORM. Every buy also pokes the big worm above, live.

      4. 04

        You can check all of it

        Your browser runs its own copy of the worm and checks the server's every second. Every hour of its life is sealed into Bitcoin. Its tests were written down before they ran. All the code is on this site.

      The tech, in plain words

      What's under the hood, and why it matters

      The short version is below. The full story, from the study to the digital worm to the launchpad, is on About; every test is on Tests; every line of code is on Docs.

      Someone buys a coin→its own worm feels a touch on its head→the big worm is poked at the coin's own spot→neurons fire, the body swims→1% fee: most of it burns $BRAINWORM

      1. A real connectome

        A real nervous system.

        A connectome is a complete map of every nerve cell and every connection between them. Ours is the published whole-body map of a three-day-old Platynereis larva: 2,675 cells, 26,881 synapses. We run it as it was mapped; nothing is invented or trained.

        Why it matters: what you see it do comes from real wiring, not from a model someone tuned to look good.

      2. One worm, live

        Everyone sees the same worm

        One simulation runs on the server, 30 steps a second, and streams to every visitor. Messages you type reach its eyes as light; pokes touch its touch sensors; a lamp in its tank closes the loop with its body.

        Why it matters: it is one shared, living thing, not a video or a random animation.

      3. Checked by your browser

        Your browser re-runs it

        The simulation is exactly reproducible, so your browser runs its own copy and compares a fingerprint of its state with the server's every second. One wrong number anywhere and the check fails, in public.

        Why it matters: nobody, not even us, can fake what the worm does. See the live check

      4. Sealed in Bitcoin

        Every hour, on the record

        Everything that happens to the worm is logged with its step. Each hour is sealed, chained to the last, and timestamped into Bitcoin. Anyone can replay any hour and get the same result.

        Why it matters: its history can't be edited after the fact. Replay an hour

      5. Tests written first

        Science, not vibes

        Each question we ask the worm had its rule written down and timestamped before it ever ran, and runs on the real wiring and on 50 scrambled copies. Passes and failures are both published.

        Why it matters: results you can trust, including the ones that failed. See every test

      6. SPAWN, the launchpad

        Your coin, its own worm, your keys

        Coins launch on Meteora's audited bonding curve: fixed supply, nothing held back, liquidity locked for good at graduation. Every coin's worm can be rebuilt from its trades in any browser. Your wallet signs every transaction; the site never holds a key. All the code is published here.

        Why it matters: a launchpad you can check end to end. Read the code

      Stated plainly

      The parts that aren't the worm

      • It can't read. Your message reaches it only as light. It understands nothing.
      • It doesn't choose. A tug shows which way its body turned, not a preference. Nothing here predicts prices.
      • The model is simpler than the animal. The transmitter is known for 79 of 2,675 cells, so almost every synapse is excitatory. Real neurons spike, inhibit and modulate.
      • Some choices are ours. The eye mapping, the light sensors' threshold, the poke cells, the swimming physics and the drawing.
      • Positions are partly placed. 1,199 cells at traced positions, 1,009 placed by segment and side, 467 fragments not drawn.
      • The body is ours. The glass outline, the swimming physics and the lamp are ours. Only the lamp feeds the body back into the brain.
      • Coins aren't the worm's. A SPAWN coin's buys poke it at a spot we pick from the coin's address, and each coin's own worm is a copy fed only that coin's trades on its bonding curve (after a coin graduates to its Meteora pool, its worm stops feeling its trades). The worm knows nothing about coins or prices, and it doesn't pick winners.
      • Where the fees go. Every trade on SPAWN pays 1%. Meteora keeps a fifth, the coin's creator gets a fifth of the rest, and the rest goes to $BRAINWORM: burned when the coin is priced in it, and for coins priced in SOL, kept in their pools until $BRAINWORM launches, then used to buy it, and what that buys is burned. Every claim and burn is a public transaction.
